\title{Can You Trust a Self-Supervised Scenario Embedding?\\
\large Diagnosing Representation Degeneration in Masked JEPA Training}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Scenario mining for ADAS validation embeds millions of unlabeled driving situations and
asks whether the embedding covers the operating domain. That question rests entirely on
label-free diagnostics, so we ask which of them can be trusted. We treat
collapse-prevention mechanisms --- stop-gradient, SIGReg \citep{lejepa2025}, and a
disposable projector --- as controlled interventions that manufacture known degeneration
modes in negative-free masked JEPA training, and measure which diagnostics distinguish
scale collapse, angular concentration, dimensional structure, and semantic loss.

Phase~A calibrates on CIFAR-10: seven conditions, five paired seeds, pre-registered
fixed-step endpoints and a pre-registered claim rule. Two results survive it. First,
geometry and semantics fail to resolve in opposite places --- across the SIGReg contrasts
every geometric comparison is large and resolvable (up to 169 units of total variance,
0.84 of mean pairwise cosine) while the probe moves by at most 0.035; and in the one
contrast whose semantic difference is unambiguous ($+0.210$ probe accuracy), neither
geometric endpoint clears the claim rule. Second, and more practically: the control
condition with no collapse prevention collapses to a point --- total variance
$2\times10^{-4}$, mean pairwise cosine $1.0000$, unscaled probe accuracy at the $0.100$
chance floor --- yet scores $0.413$ on the \emph{standardized} linear probe and retrieves
at $1.8\times$ chance. Both protocols normalize away scale by construction, and scale is
what was lost. Since standardized probing is the default SSL evaluation and cosine
similarity the default retrieval metric, a pipeline evaluated only that way cannot
distinguish a working encoder from one that has collapsed by five orders of magnitude.
We also find RankMe and the covariance participation ratio each blind to the collapse mode
the other detects, so the two are not interchangeable despite both being called
effective-rank measures.

No SIGReg configuration in our setup beat its own random initialisation; only the EMA
stop-gradient baseline learned. That is a statement about this combination --- SIGReg
paired with masked latent prediction rather than LeJEPA's multi-view invariance --- and not
about \citet{lejepa2025}. Phase~B repeats the matrix on BDD100K driving images, where the
semantic endpoint is scenario-attribute retrieval: the operation scenario mining actually
performs, and the one Phase~A finds least able to detect this failure.
\end{abstract}
