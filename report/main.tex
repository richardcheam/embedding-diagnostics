\title{Does Stop-Gradient Still Matter Under SIGReg?\\
\large A Small-Scale Ablation of JEPA Collapse Prevention}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Joint-embedding predictive architectures avoid representation collapse through a
collection of heuristics: stop-gradient, exponential-moving-average target encoders, and
masking design. LeJEPA introduces SIGReg and reports that it removes the need for these
heuristics. We test, at small scale on CIFAR-10, whether that holds as training duration
grows, and whether cheap collapse diagnostics detect degradation earlier than a linear
probe does.

A pilot run intended only to validate the measurement apparatus produced the first
substantive finding: on a control condition with no collapse prevention at all, which
collapsed completely (mean pairwise cosine 1.000), two of the four diagnostics --- effective
rank and standardized linear-probe accuracy --- \emph{rose} rather than fell. Both are
scale-invariant, and collapse here is a loss of scale. Section~\ref{sec:instrument} reports
this and the instrument changes made in response. \textbf{The main results section is to be
completed once the full-length runs finish.}
\end{abstract}
