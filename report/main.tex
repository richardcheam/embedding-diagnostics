\title{Can You Trust a Self-Supervised Scenario Embedding?\\
\large Diagnosing Representation Degeneration in Masked JEPA Training}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Scenario mining for ADAS validation embeds millions of unlabeled driving situations and
asks whether the embedding covers the operating domain. That question rests entirely on
label-free diagnostics — and this project shows those diagnostics can fail in the worst
possible direction. On a control condition with no collapse prevention, which collapsed
completely (mean pairwise cosine 1.000), two standard diagnostics \emph{rose}: effective
rank (the covariance participation ratio) and standardized linear-probe accuracy. Both are
scale-invariant, and the collapse was a loss of scale.

We therefore treat collapse-prevention mechanisms — stop-gradient, SIGReg
\citep{lejepa2025}, and a disposable projector — as controlled interventions that
manufacture known degeneration modes in negative-free masked JEPA training, and test which
label-free diagnostics distinguish scale collapse, angular concentration, dimensional
structure, and semantic loss. A calibration phase on CIFAR-10 (seven conditions, three
paired seeds, pre-registered fixed-step endpoints) is followed by the same matrix on
BDD100K driving images, where the semantic endpoint is scenario-attribute retrieval — the
operation scenario mining actually performs.

Supporting results so far, all single-seed and pending the pre-registered rerun: without a
projector, SIGReg failed to substitute for stop-gradient at every strength in its
reference range (final cosine 0.990--0.381 without stop-gradient versus 0.034--0.008 with
it), and on the one run that both learned and degraded, the expensive probe turned
\emph{before} the cheap diagnostics — the opposite of the monitoring folklore.
\textbf{Phase-2 results sections are completed as runs finish.}
\end{abstract}
