\section{Provenance of the analysis}
\label{sec:provenance}

This section exists because an earlier version of this report described a withdrawn
decision rule as pre-registered. Rather than quietly correct the text, we record what was
fixed when, so a reader can separate what the design committed to in advance from what was
chosen after seeing results. Commit hashes refer to the project repository.

\subsection{Fixed before any Phase-A run existed}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Seven conditions and their definitions, including the \texttt{none\_stopgrad} cell
        and the projector arms (\texttt{16565af}, 2026-08-14 10:10).
  \item Budget of 4{,}000 steps, checkpoints every 200.
  \item Endpoints read at the \emph{final} checkpoint, fixed a priori, with
        best-over-checkpoints reported only as selection-biased.
  \item The evaluation split, fixed by \texttt{eval\_split\_seed} independently of the
        training seed, so across-seed spread measures training variability alone.
  \item Paired Student-t analysis over five pre-declared contrasts, with EMA excluded as a
        contrast arm; explicit statements of what the interval covers, the power available
        at $n=5$, and that no multiplicity correction is applied (\texttt{96184e2},
        2026-08-14 12:44).
  \item An explicit prohibition on verdict language --- no result to be labelled
        significant, real, learned, or collapsed (same commit).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Changed before the runs, and why}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Seeds raised from three to five. Three was redesignated an exploratory pilot and
        may not be reported as claim-grade (\texttt{96184e2}).
  \item The heuristic ``claim a difference only if it clears twice the across-seed SD and
        the probe's binomial SE'' was \textbf{withdrawn} (\texttt{d566e72} in code,
        \texttt{96184e2} in the specification) on the grounds that it was not a paired
        analysis and functioned as an arbitrary decision threshold.
\end{itemize}

Phase-A data was first committed at 16:36 on the same day, roughly four hours after the
withdrawal. The paired Student-t protocol was therefore the one in force for every result
in this report.

\subsection{Introduced after results were seen --- exploratory}

None of the following was pre-registered. Each was constructed after inspecting Phase-A
outcomes and is labelled exploratory wherever it appears.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The reintroduced 2$\times$SD heuristic itself.} It was mistakenly
        reinstated in the results section and described as pre-registered
        (\texttt{5d1e47f}). The starred cells in Table~\ref{tab:phasea} are its residue and
        carry no inferential weight.
  \item \textbf{The diagnostic panel} and its absolute thresholds. Thresholds were
        calibrated on the observed separation between the collapsed control and the other
        conditions; they are descriptive of these runs and are not a validated detector.
  \item \textbf{The comparator choice} of \texttt{none\_stopgrad} as ``the weakest run that
        genuinely trains''. Selected after seeing which conditions trained.
  \item \textbf{The three-verdict scheme} (correct / cannot separate / blind) used in the
        interactive report.
  \item \textbf{The controlled degradation suite's severity grid} and the choice of which
        five transformations to include.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A measurement defect found after both campaigns}
\label{sec:probe-defect}

The probe protocol used during training was defective, and the defect is not visible in the
accuracy it reports. \texttt{scikit-learn}'s lbfgs solver stops when the gradient norm falls
below \texttt{tol}; the gradient scales with feature magnitude; so on embeddings whose
per-dimension standard deviation is around $10^{-3}$, the default $\texttt{tol}=10^{-4}$ is
satisfied almost immediately. The solver takes two or three steps, never fits, returns the
majority-class prediction, and scores at chance --- while raising \emph{no} convergence
warning.

On synthetic data whose label signal is present by construction, at the dimensionality and
scale of this project's most contracted run, the unstandardized probe scored $0.096$ against
a $0.100$ chance floor while the standardized probe scored $1.000$. Under a corrected
protocol both score $1.000$. The gap measured the optimiser, not the representation.

That this reached the reported runs is evident in the numbers themselves: on both datasets
the collapsed control's unstandardized accuracy sits exactly at its majority floor
(CIFAR-10 $0.1066$ against $0.1088$; BDD100K $0.5635$ against $0.5636$), which is what a
classifier that only ever predicts the majority class returns.

\ours{} Consequently the claim that ``the unstandardized probe correctly detects collapse
where the standardized one does not'' is \textbf{withdrawn} pending recomputation. Whether a
severely contracted representation retains decodable information is, on our present
evidence, \emph{unresolved} --- the measurement that would answer it was not functioning.
The corrected protocol selects regularisation on a validation split carved from the training
set, tightens the tolerance, and records convergence, iteration count and whether the probe
beat the trivial predictor on its own training data.
