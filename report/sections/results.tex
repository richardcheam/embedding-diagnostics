\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports Phase~A: seven conditions, five paired seeds, CIFAR-10, 4{,}000 steps,
endpoints read at the final checkpoint as pre-registered in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}.

\paragraph{Deviations from the pre-registration.} One, and it is in the strengthening
direction: the design fixed three seeds (0,~1,~2) and we ran five (0--4). Nothing else
changed --- conditions, budget, checkpoint interval, endpoint definitions, and the claim
rule are as registered. The three-seed pilot that preceded this is retained separately as
\texttt{phaseApilot} and none of its numbers appear below.

\paragraph{Correction to an earlier version of this section.} An earlier version reported
the results below under a ``pre-registered claim rule'' of twice the across-seed standard
deviation plus the probe's binomial standard error, and stated that the rule ``was fixed in
advance and we apply it as written''. \textbf{That was false.} That heuristic had been
withdrawn on 2026-08-14, roughly four hours before any Phase-A data existed, and replaced
with the paired Student-t protocol in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}. The starred cells in
Table~\ref{tab:phasea} mark that withdrawn heuristic and are retained only so the earlier
error is visible; they carry no inferential weight. Read the intervals, not the stars.
Section~\ref{sec:provenance} sets out what was fixed when.

\paragraph{Probe endpoints are under recomputation.} A defect was found in the probe
protocol used during training (Section~\ref{sec:probe-defect}): on severely
scale-contracted embeddings the optimiser's stopping rule is satisfied before it fits, so
it returns the majority-class prediction and reports chance without raising a warning.
\textbf{Every unstandardized probe number in this section is therefore provisional} and is
being recomputed from the saved encoders. The geometric endpoints --- total variance, mean
pairwise cosine, RankMe, participation ratio --- and retrieval are unaffected, since no
probe is involved in them.

\caption{\ours{} Paired mean differences over five seeds. $^\star$ = clears the

clear the rule in 8 of 10 cells; the primary semantic endpoint clears it in 2 of 5.}
\label{tab:phasea}
\end{table}

\ours{} The two axes fail to resolve in \emph{opposite} places, and this is the central
result of Phase~A. Among the four contrasts involving SIGReg, every geometric comparison is
resolvable and enormous --- 169 units of variance, 0.84 of cosine --- while the probe moves
by at most 0.035 and clears the rule once. In the one contrast where the semantic difference
is unambiguous and large (\texttt{none\_stopgrad} versus the collapsed control, $+0.210$),
neither geometric endpoint clears the rule, because \texttt{none\_stopgrad} is wildly
seed-unstable: its total variance across seeds runs 17.1, 7.5, 16.9, 50.9, 15.4.

\interp{} Read the two together. Where the geometry is measured precisely, it does not tell
us about the semantics. Where the semantics differ enormously, the geometry is too unstable
to say so. Section~\ref{sec:stress} showed this decoupling under controlled degradation of
fixed feature matrices; Phase~A shows it surviving in real training, across seeds, under a
claim rule fixed in advance.

\ours{} A caveat that limits the second half: the geometric \emph{contrast} fails, but
absolute detection does not. The collapsed control sits at cosine $1.0000$ and total
variance $2\times10^{-4}$, which no practitioner would mistake for health. What Phase~A
shows is that these diagnostics are unreliable for \emph{ranking or comparing}
configurations, not that they fail to flag total collapse.

\subsection{The standard evaluation protocol rates a dead encoder at 0.41}

\established{} Standardizing features before a probe divides each dimension by its standard
deviation, and cosine retrieval $L^2$-normalizes each vector. Both operations remove scale
by construction, so both are scale-invariant by definition --- this is a property of the
metrics, predicted a priori, not a discovery. What Phase~A contributes is the magnitude.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r r}
\toprule
\textbf{condition} & \textbf{total var.} & \textbf{cosine} & \textbf{probe uns.} & \textbf{probe std.} & \textbf{retr.\ P@10} \\
\midrule
\texttt{ema\_stopgrad}      & 87.75   & 0.386 & 0.546 & 0.541 & 0.358 \\
\texttt{none\_stopgrad}     & 21.57   & 0.840 & 0.316 & 0.347 & 0.186 \\
\texttt{sigreg\_nostopgrad} & 0.693   & 0.850 & 0.338 & 0.388 & 0.216 \\
\texttt{none\_nostopgrad}   & 0.0002  & 1.000 & \textbf{0.107} & \textbf{0.413} & \textbf{0.184} \\
\midrule
\emph{chance}               & ---     & ---   & 0.100 & 0.100 & 0.100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\ours{} Final-checkpoint means over five seeds. The bottom row is the
no-collapse-prevention control: total variance $4\times10^5$ times below the baseline and
mean pairwise cosine at unity. The unscaled probe correctly reports it dead. The
standardized probe reports 0.413.}
\label{tab:blindness}
\end{table}

\ours{} The control encoder is collapsed to a point. Its unscaled probe accuracy is
$0.107$ against a chance floor of $0.100$ --- correctly dead. Its standardized probe
accuracy is $0.413$, which ranks it \emph{second of seven} --- above five of the six
other conditions, behind only the EMA baseline. Its retrieval P@10 is $0.184$,
$1.8\times$ chance, and
statistically indistinguishable from \texttt{none\_stopgrad}'s $0.186$ (paired difference
$+0.002$, far inside the claim rule).

\interp{} The mechanism is the one anticipated in the probe module's documentation:
standardization rescales the collapsed representation's residual numerical noise back to
unit variance, and that noise is still a deterministic function of the input, so a linear
model reads it. $L^2$-normalization does the same for retrieval. Both protocols surgically
remove the exact axis along which this failure occurred.

\interp{} This is the finding with the most practical reach, because both blind protocols
are the defaults. Standardized linear probing is the standard SSL evaluation, and cosine
similarity is the default in essentially every vector database. An embedding pipeline
evaluated only that way cannot distinguish a working encoder from one that has collapsed by
five orders of magnitude in scale. The correction is cheap --- log total variance and the
unscaled probe alongside --- but it has to be done deliberately.

\ours{} For scenario mining specifically the implication is uncomfortable. Retrieval is the
operation the application actually performs, and it is the endpoint least able to detect
this failure. Whether the effect survives on driving data is Phase~B.

\subsection{Neither rank measure is sufficient alone}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
\textbf{condition} & \textbf{total var.} & \textbf{participation ratio} & \textbf{RankMe} \\
\midrule
\texttt{ema\_stopgrad}      & 87.75  & 26.90 & 85.72 \\
\texttt{sigreg\_nostopgrad} & 0.693  & 16.63 & \textbf{39.60} \\
\texttt{none\_nostopgrad}   & 0.0002 & \textbf{34.98} & 1.12 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\ours{} Each rank measure is blind to one collapse mode. Bold entries are the
misleading readings.}
\label{tab:rank}
\end{table}

\ours{} On the collapsed-to-a-point control, RankMe correctly reports $1.12$ while the
participation ratio reports $34.98$ --- higher than the healthy baseline's $26.90$. On
\texttt{sigreg\_nostopgrad}, whose total variance is 127 times below baseline, the
participation ratio correctly drops to $16.63$ while RankMe reports $39.60$.

\interp{} The two disagree because they are computed differently: the participation ratio
uses the \emph{centered} covariance spectrum, so it cannot see a collapse to a non-zero
constant, while RankMe uses raw singular values and does. Conversely SIGReg keeps its
residual variance isotropic even as it shrinks, which flattens the raw spectrum and keeps
RankMe high. Each measure is blind to the mode the other catches. Total variance flags both.

\ours{} If a single label-free number must be chosen, our results favour total variance over
either rank measure --- but the honest reading of Table~\ref{tab:rank} is that no single
number was sufficient here, and RankMe and the participation ratio should not be treated as
interchangeable despite both being described as effective-rank measures.

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\ours{} Unscaled probe accuracy against each run's own random initialisation,

\end{table}

\ours{} Only \texttt{ema\_stopgrad} learns. Every SIGReg arm finishes below its own random
initialisation, and so does stop-gradient without SIGReg. The projector changes the geometry
substantially --- without stop-gradient it raises total variance from $0.69$ to $34.65$, a
50-fold difference that clears the claim rule --- but it does not convert that into probe
accuracy, where its effect is $-0.016$ and unclaimed.

\paragraph{Status of the pre-registered falsification.} The registered criterion was that
the projector hypothesis is falsified if \texttt{proj\_sigreg\_nostopgrad} collapses as
badly as \texttt{sigreg\_nostopgrad} on all seeds. It does not: the projector prevents the
scale collapse decisively and on every seed. \ours{} The hypothesis therefore survives its
registered test on the geometric axis and gains no support on the semantic axis, which is
the same dissociation as Table~\ref{tab:phasea}. \interp{} We note that the criterion we
registered was purely geometric, and Phase~A is the reason we now think that was the wrong
thing to register.

\subsection{What these results do not show}

\interp{} That no SIGReg configuration beat random initialisation here is \textbf{not}
evidence against \citet{lejepa2025}. We pair SIGReg with I-JEPA-style masked latent
prediction; LeJEPA pairs it with multi-view invariance across eight augmented views, with no
predictor and no teacher-student network. These are different systems sharing one component,
and our projector is a project-specific MLP buffer inspired by LeJEPA's, not a reproduction
of it. The result is a statement about this combination at this scale, not about SIGReg and
not about LeJEPA's claim.

\interp{} Nor is the diagnostic finding established beyond this setting. It rests on one
dataset, one architecture, one budget, and a collapse produced by removing stop-gradient
rather than arising spontaneously. The scale-invariance argument that explains it is a
property of the metrics and should generalise; the magnitudes need not. Phase~B tests
whether the same blindness appears on driving data, where retrieval is the endpoint that
matters.
