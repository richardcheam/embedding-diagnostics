Embedding diagnostics Research synthesis / experiments closed

What geometry reveals.
What utility checks add.

An embedding turns an image into a list of numbers so that similar images can be found nearby. This project tests whether simple measurements of those numbers tell us how useful the representation is.

Read the synthesis ↓
Problem
During training, image embeddings can shrink or become too similar to one another. Can simple diagnostic measurements tell us whether they still support classification and search?
Approach
Combine controlled JEPA training with pretrained-vector interventions, learned compression, numerical storage checks and authenticated image–caption evaluation.
Main finding
[interpretation] Geometry, label prediction, neighbour identity and recorded-positive coverage measure different properties. Read their responses together, with source and uncertainty qualifications.
01 / Geometry and utility

What do geometry diagnostics reveal?
What can they miss?

Geometry describes the distribution of vectors. A probe asks whether a fitted classifier can recover labels. Retrieval asks which examples appear nearby. These questions need separate answers.

[established] Positive rescaling contracts total variance quadratically while preserving cosine ordering and scale-invariant rank measures. Adding a common offset preserves centered variance; standardization removes that offset. These are mathematical properties, not newly discovered failure modes.

[ours] The qualified pretrained study reproduces those responses. Severe noise raises dimensionality readings while weather retrieval approaches its chance floor. A high rank reading alone does not establish useful neighbours.

[interpretation] Raw RankMe and centered participation ratio can disagree because they measure different spectra. Neither supplies a universal quality verdict. Absolute variance thresholds from the unnormalized training study do not transfer to unit-normalized EmbeddingGemma vectors.

02 / Dimensional compression

Does a smaller representation
lose the same utility everywhere?

Compare trained Matryoshka prefixes with generic post-hoc PCA at the same dimensional budget. Both are normalized after reduction; PCA is centered and fitted on training vectors only.

[ours] MRL 512 and 256 retain much of the measured native utility on this sample; 128 has larger losses for several endpoints. PCA is not uniformly better or worse. For example, PCA 128 has higher weather balanced accuracy than MRL 128, while MRL 128 has slightly higher weather P@10.

[interpretation] Lower nominal dimension or raw rank does not imply a proportional loss of downstream utility. The usefulness of learned compression is familiar; this study adds a qualified comparison of diagnostic readings and several utility endpoints. It does not introduce a new compression method or a general quality predictor.

[established] MRL takes the learned leading dimensions and L2 renormalizes. PCA and MRL also differ in centering and coordinate construction, so this comparison does not isolate the cause of their differences. 03 / Numerical neighbourhoods

When does numerical perturbation
change neighbour identity?

A small score error can reorder nearly tied candidates. Storage precision, similarity arithmetic and approximate search are distinct interventions. This study tests the first two using exact search; it does not test ANN.

__C3_TABLE__

[ours] Native FP16 gallery storage changes five of 983 top-10 sets; INT8 gallery storage changes 60. FP32 normalization and scoring change none. Under the frozen common-offset stress, FP16 changes 195 sets despite a smaller absolute score error than native FP16.

[ours] All native FP16 changes lie in the lowest reference-margin bin. INT8 gallery changes are distributed 55/5/0/0 across the four deterministic bins. Reconstruction-based sufficient conditions cover 576 native FP16-gallery queries, with no observed exception; they cover no INT8 or stressed queries.

[interpretation] The boundary margin matters alongside error size. The bounds are mathematical sufficient conditions checked empirically, not rigorous floating-point certification. Retrospective observed score errors cannot serve as prospective certificates.

[ours] Among the 195 stressed identity changes, weather P@10 is unchanged for 131 queries, scene for 119, and time of day for 149. Entering and departing label counts avoid arbitrary neighbour pairings. [interpretation] Stable coarse labels do not establish stable instance semantics, object layout or safety relevance; these were not measured.

Storage transforms operate on cached FP32 model outputs. Most comparisons reconstruct and score in FP64; the arithmetic-only condition normalizes and accumulates in FP32. No low-precision encoder inference occurred. Deltas are percentage points relative to the native or stressed reference, respectively. Margin evidence · Qualifications.

04 / Recorded positives and coverage

Does preserving one positive
preserve ranking and caption coverage?

Original-release COCO image–caption pairs supply finer relevance than driving attributes: 1,000 frozen image groups and five human-authored captions each. This is independent evaluation sampling with the same encoder, not an independent encoder replication.

Hit@10 means at least one recorded positive appears in the top ten. Hit@1 tests first-rank recovery. Image→text caption recall@10 measures how many of an image's five captions are recovered. Category P@10 is a separate image-only comparison over 75 eligible object categories; it is not paired-caption relevance.

__COCO_TABLE__

[ours] At 128d, image→text Hit@10 falls 2.50 percentage points, while Hit@1 falls 10.60 points and recovery of the five captions falls 11.34 points. At 256d those differences are −0.30, −0.70 and −1.74 points. Top-10 identity overlap is approximately .84 at 256d and .65 at 128d in both directions.

[interpretation] Intervals crossing zero do not demonstrate equivalence. Ranking and coverage losses can be larger than a positive-hit summary suggests.

[ours] The native circular pairing control leaves vectors and geometry fixed but reduces Hit@10 to 1.22% for text→image and 0.20% for image→text. [interpretation] Marginal geometry cannot establish paired alignment. Category and paired retrieval broadly lose utility under stronger compression here; there is no strong reversal establishing preservation of coarse utility alongside destroyed pairing.

[interpretation] Directions use different caption-role prefixes: query text for text→image and document text for image→text. Directional differences cannot isolate modality asymmetry. Three dimensionalities do not validate a general predictor of representation quality.

Training study / motivation

Find similar driving scenes.
First, trust the representation.

Imagine searching a large collection of road images for scenes similar to a rainy night on a city street. An embedding-based search system compares numerical representations of the images instead of reading a written description of each one.

Driving image→Image model→Training embedding: 192 numbers→Similar images

The risk

A model trained without human labels can reduce its prediction loss without producing a useful representation. Its outputs might become very small, point in nearly the same direction, or vary along very few dimensions. These are geometric warning signs, but they are different from directly measuring whether information can be recovered.

The research question

Can measurements made without labels tell us which embeddings remain useful for classification and driving-scene search?

This is a diagnostic study. The aim is to test the measurements under controlled conditions, rather than build a state-of-the-art driving model.

Training study / approach

Create controlled failure modes.
Measure them the same way.

Train seven variants of one image model, changing only the mechanisms intended to prevent degeneration. Compare their embeddings using the same data, training schedule, and evaluation procedure.

How the model learns

The model follows a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA): hide parts of an image and predict their internal representations from the visible parts. An encoder is the neural network that converts images into embeddings. A predictor is the training component that attempts the hidden-region prediction. [1]

All seven variants use the same training loop. One is an intentionally unprotected control: it removes the mechanisms being tested so the diagnostics can be examined on a representation that degenerates.

Stop-gradient
Prevents the target side of a prediction from being updated through that prediction’s gradient.
EMA target
A separate target encoder updated by an exponential moving average, a slowly changing copy of the main encoder.
SIGReg
Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization: a published loss that encourages the embedding distribution to resemble a standard Gaussian. [2]
Projector
A temporary neural-network layer between the encoder and SIGReg. Evaluation reads the encoder, not this layer.

Training study / data

Driving photographs.
Three attributes to evaluate.

The image encoder trains without category labels. Labels are used later to fit the evaluation classifier and score classification and retrieval on held-out images. This allows a comparison of diagnostics that need no labels against tasks whose outcomes we can measure.

Driving-scene evaluation

BDD100K

The Berkeley DeepDrive dataset contains 100,000 driving videos. [3] This study uses their annotated still images, rather than video sequences.

Why use it? Labels describe weather, scene type (such as a city street or highway), and time of day. They provide measurable checks on driving-image similarity.

The results below use BDD100K. Single frames describe visual context; they do not measure vehicle motion or driving behaviour.

Three questions, three kinds of measurement

Geometry / no labels

How are the embeddings distributed?

Total variance measures their spread. Mean pairwise cosine measures how similarly their vectors point. RankMe and participation ratio describe different aspects of their dimensional structure. RankMe follows Garrido et al. [4]

Classification / labels used

Can a simple classifier recover a label?

A linear probe freezes the encoder and fits a simple classifier on its embeddings. Accuracy measures correct predictions; balanced accuracy averages recall across sufficiently represented classes.

Retrieval / labels used

Do the nearest images share an attribute?

Find the ten closest embeddings by cosine similarity. Precision at 10 (P@10) is the fraction sharing the query image’s weather, scene type, or time-of-day label. It measures attribute agreement, not complete scene equivalence.

Class frequencies matter: always guessing the most common label can score well on an imbalanced dataset. The figures therefore show the majority-class accuracy baseline and the expected score of random retrieval.

BDD100K: understand the comparison

EMA reference: the comparison model uses a slowly updated target encoder and stop-gradient. Unprotected control: the same architecture and training loop, with both mechanisms removed and no SIGReg regularization.

Classification test: freeze each image encoder and fit a simple classifier, called a linear probe, to predict time of day from its embeddings. Select regularization on a training validation split and fit with a tight stopping tolerance and a high iteration limit. Accuracy is the percentage of held-out images classified correctly.

How uncertain are these differences?

Compare the same seed, then average the differences.

Each row changes one mechanism. For each of the five seeds, subtract model B’s score from model A’s score: dᵢ = score(A, i) − score(B, i). This pairing removes variation shared by both runs, rather than treating them as unrelated experiments.

Read from the zero line. Right means a higher score for A; left means a lower score for A. The small dots are the five seed differences, the diamond is their mean, and the horizontal line is the 95% interval. A line spanning zero includes differences in both directions. A wider line means a less precise estimate under this protocol.

What the interval covers

For the five differences, let d̄ be their mean and sd their sample standard deviation. The interval is d̄ ± 2.776 × sd / √5, using Student’s t distribution with four degrees of freedom. One percentage point means, for example, a change from 90% to 91%.

These intervals describe training-seed variation on one fixed evaluation split. They do not cover a different dataset, image split, or architecture. The five comparisons were declared before these runs; no correction for multiple comparisons is applied. The classifier-fitting settings were selected after training. EMA remains a reference, rather than a paired mechanism contrast, because it changes multiple factors. [5]

How are dimensionality measurements calculated?
Technical note / Rank diagnostics

How many directions
carry the representation?

An embedding has 192 coordinates, but these can move together. If every image varies along one line, 192 coordinates describe only one independent direction. These measurements give a smooth count of how broadly the representation uses its directions, rather than counting every tiny nonzero direction equally.

Start with the matrix of image embeddings.

Stack n images as the rows of a matrix Z ∈ ℝn×d, with d = 192 coordinates per image. A spectrum lists the strength of independent, perpendicular directions in that matrix. Both measurements turn a spectrum into relative shares, then ask how evenly those shares are distributed.

01 / Participation ratio

Count directions of variation around the mean.

Subtract the mean embedding μ from each row to obtain X. The covariance matrix is C = XᵀX / (n − 1). Its eigenvalues λᵢ ≥ 0 are the variances along its independent directions. Their sum is total variance.

qi=λi∑jλj PR=(∑iλi)2∑iλi2=1∑iqi2

Why this makes sense: squaring the shares makes a dominant direction weigh heavily. If one direction holds everything, the sum of squared shares is 1 and PR is 1. If k directions each hold 1/k, that sum is 1/k and PR is k. Unequal shares give an intermediate count.

02 / RankMe

Count directions in the raw matrix.

Use Z without subtracting its mean. Singular value decomposition gives strengths σᵢ ≥ 0 for its perpendicular directions. Normalize these strengths into shares pᵢ, then calculate their entropy. [4]

pi=σi∑jσj RankMe=exp(−∑ipilnpi)

Why exponentiate entropy? One direction has entropy 0, so its count is exp(0) = 1. With k equal shares, entropy is ln(k); exponentiating returns k. This puts the measurement in units of directions. Here ln is the natural logarithm and zero shares contribute zero to entropy.

A worked example: two directions, one much stronger.

Illustration only, not experiment data. Take a zero-mean matrix with singular values (3, 1, 0, 0). Because it is already centered, we can isolate the difference in weighting:

  1. Covariance eigenvalues are proportional to squared singular values: (9, 1, 0, 0). Variance shares are (0.9, 0.1, 0, 0), giving PR = 1 / (0.9² + 0.1²) ≈ 1.22.
  2. RankMe uses singular-value shares (0.75, 0.25, 0, 0), giving exp[−0.75 ln(0.75) − 0.25 ln(0.25)] ≈ 1.75.

Both counts lie between one and two, because two directions are present but contribute unequally. They differ because variance squares the singular values, while RankMe uses their unsquared strengths.

What these counts cannot tell you

Centering matters. Adding the same offset vector to every embedding leaves centered covariance and PR unchanged. Raw RankMe can change because that shared mean contributes to the singular-value spectrum.

Magnitude matters separately. Multiplying every embedding by a small positive number leaves both normalized spectra unchanged, while total variance falls by the square of that number. A representation can therefore shrink without either count falling. Neither count identifies which directions encode useful labels; read them alongside variance, classification, and retrieval.

The equations show the ideal nonzero-spectrum case. The implementation adds 10−7 to RankMe’s normalized singular values for numerical stability and returns 1 for an exactly zero spectrum in either measurement. That convention is not evidence of useful information. [5]
